Cybersecurity Analyst

A cybersecurity analyst, also called an information security analyst, is responsible for protecting an organization's computer networks and data from threats. This career combines technical problem-solving with constant vigilance, making it one of the most in-demand roles in technology today.

What they do

An information security analyst plans and carries out security measures to protect an organization's computer networks and systems. They watch for unusual activity that could signal a breach, respond to incidents when they occur, and work to close gaps before attackers can exploit them. Their work is critical because a single missed alert can lead to data theft, financial loss, or damage to a company's reputation.

  • Monitor network traffic and security dashboards for suspicious alerts or anomalies
  • Investigate and respond to security incidents, then document findings in a report
  • Run vulnerability scanning and penetration tests to find weaknesses in the system
  • Update and maintain security tools such as firewalls, intrusion detection systems, and anti-malware software

Skills you need

Technical skills

  • Knowledge of network security protocols and architecture
  • Ability to analyze system logs and detect anomalies
  • Experience with incident response tools and procedures

People skills

  • Clear written and verbal communication to explain threats to non-technical staff
  • Teamwork and the ability to work well under pressure during a breach

How to get started

  1. Earn a high school diploma, ideally with computer science or math classes
  2. Earn a bachelor's degree in cybersecurity, computer science, or a related field
  3. Earn the CompTIA Security+ certification to validate your baseline security skills
  4. Apply for an entry-level position such as Junior Security Analyst or SOC Analyst

Certifications and credentials

The two most recognized certifications in this field are:

  • CompTIA Security+ – offered by CompTIA, this is the entry-level credential that validates core security skills
  • CISSP (Certified Information Systems Security Professional) – offered by ISC2, this is a senior-level credential that requires five years of experience

Pay and job outlook

According to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Outlook Handbook, the median annual wage for information security analysts was $129,180 in May 2025. The BLS projects employment in this field to grow 21% from 2025 to 2035, which is much faster than the average for all occupations. This strong growth is driven by the increasing number of cyberattacks and the growing amount of sensitive data that organizations must protect.

Where you could work

Information security analysts are hired across many industries. The finance and insurance sector is one of the top employers, with companies like Bank of America and Fidelity Investments maintaining large security teams. Technology companies such as Microsoft and CrowdStrike also hire analysts to protect their own infrastructure and to build security products. Government agencies and defense contractors, including Booz Allen Hamilton and Northrop Grumman, are major employers as well.
